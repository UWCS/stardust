There aren't many great things about being a second year, CS260 ❌, the U1 Bus ❌, CS276 ❌ (okay I lied about that last one its actually pretty fun ✅).

Once the light drains from your eyes after your first term at Warwick theres no amount of Pop, Skool Dayz or Euphoria you can do to bring it back.

I used to think that, then I was initiated into The Cult of Costco.

You see, the first challenge is making it into Costco, as only a select few hold the keys to enter (obtaining your own card as a student is a highly gatekept secret), but once you're escorted past the Guardians of the Wholesale™: oh. my. god.

The first thing that hits you is the sheer scale of the place. Shelves towering 20 feet into the air, stacked with enough stock to sustain a small nation through a nuclear winter.

And the variety is absurd. One minute you're browsing 75-inch TVs, the next you're looking at clothes, and before you know it you're face to face with a tube of minced beef roughly the size of your forearm. Why does anyone need that much beef? I don't know. Did I briefly consider buying it? Absolutely.

Then there's the bakery. Mountains of muffins, cookies and pastries, enough baked goods to kill a diabetic stone dead. Everything is sold in quantities that suggest you're either feeding a family of twelve or preparing to open your own Greggs.

But the real miracle is the alcohol aisle.

I'm convinced the people setting the prices here have never actually purchased alcohol before. The booze is so ridiculously cheap it makes a Smack vodka Red Bull look like a luxury purchase. After spending the last year paying Warwick prices for drinks, it felt like discovering some sort of financial loophole.

And the fun doesn't stop once you've checked out.

Ladies and gentlemen, allow me to introduce you to the humble £1.50 Costco hot dog.

"What is it made of?" Don't know.

"How many calories?" Don't care.

"Can I have onions with that?" F*CK YES YOU CAN. AT NO EXTRA COST.

A hot dog for £1.50. In this economy. I genuinely don't understand how they're making any money. At this point I'm convinced Costco is operating as some sort of charitable organisation, I'm suspicious, whats the catch? 🤔

But just when you think you've experienced everything this magical place has to offer, you're about to leave the car park when a number catches your eye.

**166 PENCE PER LITRE.**

SORRYYYY??????

What do you mean petrol is a whole 10p cheaper than the Coventry average? How on earth does that work? You're telling me that after selling me a suspiciously cheap hot dog, enough alcohol to put a small village in hospital and a lifetime supply of baked goods, you're ALSO offering to fill my car up at a discount???

I think I finally understand why people join cults.

Anyway, for the first time since starting second year, I think a little bit of light has returned to my eyes. It might just be the reflection of Costco's fluorescent warehouse lighting, but I'll take what I can get.

If anyone has a Costco membership, please get in touch. I'm getting hotdog withdrawals and I need my fix...

#### 🕓 Tue 20th Oct 4pm - 5pm  📍 [CS1.01](https://link.mazemap.com/VrUCnrJB)
#### *its not the size of the weight its how you use it ;)*
## Is Bigger Always Better? The Surprising (and dangerous?) Science Behind Large Language Models

How does a model trained to predict the next word become capable of translating languages, writing code, and tackling unfamiliar tasks? And can we keep making AI better simply by making it bigger? This talk explores the ideas behind the rise of large language models, from scaling laws (model size, data, and computing power) to learning from just a few examples in a prompt. We will investigate why some abilities seem to appear suddenly and perhaps dangerously unpredictably, whether these breakthroughs are as mysterious as they look, and how bigger models or more data can sometimes make performance worse. Along the way, we will encounter models that seemingly memorise before learning to generalise, and discuss what these surprising behaviours reveal about machine learning.
